Target and view

Keyboard: ↑ ↓ ← → move the view. Shift + → rotates clockwise, Shift + ← counter-clockwise. fn + ↑ zooms in, fn + ↓ zooms out (Page Up / Page Down). Hold any of them for smooth, steady motion.

The overlay reads Aladin’s live viewport scale, so zooming with the mouse keeps every rectangle correctly scaled.

Constellations

Overlay statusOff

Constellation data is loaded once. Lines and names are drawn into the sky image itself, so they stay locked to the stars while you pan, zoom, or rotate; on the SIN globe they follow the sphere and stop at its edge. Figures use a lightweight overlay. None of this alters the camera, OAG, and mosaic overlays.

Ground view

Shows the sky as you see it from your location: ground below the horizon, compass points, and the sky turning as time passes. Drag or use the arrow keys to look around; the horizon stays level.

Solar system right now

Click a row to turn toward it. The time buttons on the sky (or Space) pause and resume time.

Stars use your location and the time above (accurate to about 0.01°); planets and the Moon are good to about 0.03°. The Sun and Moon are drawn at true size, but never smaller than a few pixels. The landscape is illustrative; the true horizon is at 0° altitude. Camera, OAG and mosaic frames stay true to the sky and turn with it.

Telescope and reducer

Seestar S30 / S30 Pro presets select the built-in telephoto camera and native optics automatically. Frames use portrait orientation: S30 ≈ 1.20° × 2.13°; S30 Pro ≈ 2.24° × 3.99°. Compare the two using separate comparison setups.

The Apertura CarbonStar reducing coma corrector is 0.95×. Celestron’s classic SCT reducer is 0.63×; dedicated EdgeHD reducers for the 8, 9.25, 11, and 14 are 0.70×.
